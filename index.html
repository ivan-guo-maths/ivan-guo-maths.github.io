I am the Director of the Monash Centre for Quantitative Finance and Investment Strategies (CQFIS).

My research focuses on optimal transport, stochastic control and mathematical finance, with applications in model calibration, robust finance and stochastic games. I enjoy discovering unexpected connections between different areas of mathematics and exploring the new applications they open up.

I am also passionate about mathematical problem solving and take an active role in Olympiad mathematics through contest organisation, problem setting and student mentoring.

Research

My research combines probability, optimal transport, convex duality and stochastic control, with applications in mathematical finance. Particular interests include (semi)martingale optimal transport and how constraints on distributions shape stochastic dynamics and the geometry of optimal solutions. I am also interested in how information unfolds over time, how it shapes decisions, and how the actions of interacting agents influence one another.

More broadly, I enjoy problems with simple formulations and rich mathematical structure, especially when abstract mathematical objects have concrete probabilistic or physical interpretations. These connections guide both the questions I ask and the methods I use. My work in finance spans volatility modelling and calibration, robust pricing and hedging, portfolio optimisation, market making and stochastic games.
